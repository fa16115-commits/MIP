# Draft module review register

All PMO and KPI definitions below are Draft. Source concepts were inspected, but these level rubrics are authored adaptations and require approval. No critical gates or stage thresholds are seeded.

## PMO, Portfolio & Program Management

Draft coverage for review, not a complete certified framework. Source describes a 0–4 assessment scale; this proposed 1–5 adaptation requires explicit approval. Launch-control gates are not automatically maturity caps.

### Mandate and governance

#### Mandate and service authority

How consistently does the organization demonstrate mandate and service authority?

Evidence: Approved mandate, service catalogue and escalation decisions

Source: [PMO Establishment Playbook (July 2026)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/158w-d7u-UXTCgp58kUB-Qkq8Hrbvkr_k/view), 3.2 Phase 2; 7 PMO Service Catalogue. Verification: source inspected 27 September 2026; rubric approval pending.

1. PMO authority and services are informal.
2. A sponsor endorses a basic service list, with unresolved authority boundaries.
3. An approved mandate defines customers, services, authority and escalation and is applied.
4. Service reviews test mandate effectiveness and resolve authority conflicts.
5. Demonstrated outcomes drive periodic redesign of services and authority.

#### Decision rights and stage gates

How consistently does the organization demonstrate decision rights and stage gates?

Evidence: Delegation matrix, gate criteria, decision logs

Source: [PMO Establishment Playbook (July 2026)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/158w-d7u-UXTCgp58kUB-Qkq8Hrbvkr_k/view), 5 Governance Framework; 8 Project Lifecycle and Stage Gates. Verification: source inspected 27 September 2026; rubric approval pending.

1. Approvals occur without defined rights or criteria.
2. Some gate reviews occur but responsibilities and evidence vary.
3. Documented decision rights and evidence-based gates operate consistently.
4. Exceptions and gate effectiveness are measured and acted on.
5. Gate design is improved using outcomes while preserving accountability.

### Portfolio and programs

#### Portfolio selection and balancing

How consistently does the organization demonstrate portfolio selection and balancing?

Evidence: Demand register, selection records and capacity scenarios

1. Projects enter the portfolio without consistent selection.
2. Basic prioritization exists without reliable capacity checks.
3. Documented criteria, evidence and capacity inform portfolio decisions.
4. Trade-offs and portfolio performance are reviewed using reliable data.
5. Selection and balancing are refined from realized value and scenario evidence.

How consistently does the organization demonstrate benefits ownership and realization?

### Delivery and assurance

#### Integrated delivery baselines

How consistently does the organization demonstrate integrated delivery baselines?

Evidence: Scope, schedule and cost baselines with update records

1. Scope, cost and schedule are disconnected or absent.
2. Basic plans exist but change and forecast disciplines vary.
3. Approved integrated baselines are maintained through controlled updates.
4. Forecasts and variances trigger documented recovery decisions.
5. Measured planning and delivery outcomes improve estimation and controls.

How consistently does the organization demonstrate risk, issue and change control?

Evidence: Risk register, issue log, approved change decisions

4. Exposure, aging and change trends drive management intervention.

#### Assurance and acceptance

How consistently does the organization demonstrate assurance and acceptance?

Evidence: Assurance findings, closure evidence and acceptance records

## Performance Management & KPI

Draft coverage for review, not a complete certified framework. Checklist evaluates KPI quality, not the complete performance management lifecycle. This draft requires coverage review before approval.

### Design and alignment

#### Clarity

How consistently does the organization demonstrate clarity?

Evidence: KPI dictionary and stakeholder interpretation checks

Source: [KPI Quality Evaluation – 8-Point Assessment Checklist](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1cRjMJZh-WW5GCo6bqfd0gyczInBAjbLIMZKlJc75ZMo/edit), Clarity. Verification: source inspected 27 September 2026; rubric approval pending.

1. Metric names obscure what is measured.
2. Definitions exist for some metrics but interpretation varies.
3. Each KPI has an unambiguous definition and documented calculation.
4. Reviews detect and correct interpretation and formula inconsistencies.
5. Use evidence continually improves definition clarity without breaking history.

#### Relevance

How consistently does the organization demonstrate relevance?

Evidence: Objective-to-KPI mappings and decision records

Source: [KPI Quality Evaluation – 8-Point Assessment Checklist](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1cRjMJZh-WW5GCo6bqfd0gyczInBAjbLIMZKlJc75ZMo/edit), Relevance. Verification: source inspected 27 September 2026; rubric approval pending.

1. KPIs have no demonstrated link to objectives.
2. Some KPIs reference goals without decision use.
3. KPIs are linked to relevant objectives and decision needs.
4. Reviews assess usefulness and retire measures that do not support decisions.
5. Changing strategy and evaluated decision outcomes drive controlled KPI redesign.

#### Balance

How consistently does the organization demonstrate balance?

Evidence: Indicator portfolio with input, output and outcome coverage

Source: [KPI Quality Evaluation – 8-Point Assessment Checklist](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1cRjMJZh-WW5GCo6bqfd0gyczInBAjbLIMZKlJc75ZMo/edit), Balance. Verification: source inspected 27 September 2026; rubric approval pending.

1. The indicator set overlooks results or outcomes.
2. Different indicator types exist with major unexplained gaps.
3. The indicator set deliberately covers effort, delivery results and outcomes.
4. Reviews identify imbalances and their effects on decisions.
5. Outcome evidence drives sustained refinement of the indicator mix.

How consistently does the organization demonstrate comparability?

Evidence: Units, definitions, restatements and trend records

#### Data availability

How consistently does the organization demonstrate data availability?

Evidence: Named data sources, collection records and reconciliations

5. Measured data problems drive sustained improvements to source reliability.

#### Timeliness

How consistently does the organization demonstrate timeliness?

Evidence: Reporting calendar and timestamped delivery records

1. KPI data arrives too late for its intended decisions.
2. Reporting dates exist but delays are frequent and unexplained.
3. Collection and reporting schedules align with decision timing.
4. Delay causes and service performance are reviewed and corrected.
5. Evidence of decision needs guides sustained improvements to reporting timing.

### Accountability and improvement

#### Ownership

How consistently does the organization demonstrate ownership?

Evidence: KPI ownership register and accountability records

Source: [KPI Quality Evaluation – 8-Point Assessment Checklist](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1cRjMJZh-WW5GCo6bqfd0gyczInBAjbLIMZKlJc75ZMo/edit), Ownership. Verification: source inspected 27 September 2026; rubric approval pending.

1. No one is accountable for updating and using KPIs.
2. Informal owners exist but responsibilities remain incomplete.
3. Named owners maintain KPIs and are accountable for their use.
4. Reviews address ownership gaps and overdue decisions or updates.
5. Accountability arrangements evolve using evidence of performance improvement.

#### Actionability

How consistently does the organization demonstrate actionability?

Evidence: Performance review decisions, corrective actions and results

Source: [KPI Quality Evaluation – 8-Point Assessment Checklist](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1cRjMJZh-WW5GCo6bqfd0gyczInBAjbLIMZKlJc75ZMo/edit), Actionability. Verification: source inspected 27 September 2026; rubric approval pending.

1. Metric changes lead to no defined response.
2. Occasional actions occur without consistent follow-up.
3. Performance reviews translate relevant changes into owned actions.
4. Action completion and effects are assessed and escalated when necessary.
5. Verified intervention outcomes continually improve performance decisions.
